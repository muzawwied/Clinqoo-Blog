How to Use Gemini and ChatGPT to Build an SME Landing Page in 2026

A practical 2026 guide to using Gemini and ChatGPT for an SME landing page: prompts, visuals, copy, and a pre-publish checklist.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

Plenty of small-business owners in Indonesia already chat with AI for captions and posters, yet they still delay a landing page because it sounds like designer-plus-developer work. In 2026 that picture has shifted. Microsoft’s Work Trend Index found that 33 percent of Indonesian workers now count as Frontier Professionals—advanced AI users—more than twice the global average. The tools you already open every day are strong enough to assemble a clean offer page if the sequence is clear: research first, write the copy, generate visuals, then lay out the page. This article walks through that sequence without the jargon that usually stalls people.

Start with a promise you can verify, not with a mood board. Write down who you serve, the problem they feel this week, and what they get within seven days of filling a form or tapping WhatsApp. Paste that note into ChatGPT and ask for a concrete outline: “Draft a one-screen landing page for [business], audience [who], promise [what], proof [testimonial or number], and one WhatsApp button.” Request three heading tones—calm, firm, and friendly—then pick one. OpenAI pushes the same idea in ChatGPT Sites: begin with a small, focused use case instead of a full site that tries to solve every problem at once.

Once the copy skeleton exists, move to Gemini for visuals. Google reports that Indonesia is the most creative Gemini visual-AI market in Southeast Asia, generating nearly nine million custom images a day, with one in three local prompts purely creative. Use that energy with discipline. Do not ask for “a nice food product photo.” Specify angle, lighting, a neutral backdrop, 16:9 for the hero and 1:1 for service cards, plus short on-image text in Indonesian. Official Gemini docs support id-ID on current image models and stress more legible text rendering. Generate three variants, pick the one that does not scream “AI stock,” then iterate: “add a little mess, reduce plastic shine, match our brand palette.”

Combine both outputs in a builder. If you use ChatGPT Sites or Codex, mention @Sites, paste the outline, upload Gemini images, and constrain the layout: one hero, three benefits, one social-proof block, one FAQ, one CTA. If you assemble the page in a website builder such as Clincoo, paste headings and paragraphs section by section, then test the phone preview before you touch the desktop view. Do not let the model rewrite the whole brand. Lock the colors, type, and greeting you already use on Instagram. The pages that win in 2026 are rarely the most “futuristic”; they are the ones a first-time visitor understands in eight seconds on a small screen.

Three mistakes keep showing up. First, vague prompts produce marketplace-ad headings. Fix them by adding the city, package price, and stock limit. Second, hero images stuffed with decorative type crack on mobile; keep words in HTML and let the photo carry the object. Third, CTAs scatter: Buy, Sign up, and Chat Admin on the same row. Choose one action. Google projects that generative AI can unlock tens of trillions of rupiah in Indonesia’s creative economy precisely because it saves repetitive hours—not because it replaces the owner’s judgment. You still decide which promise is allowed to go live.

Before you publish, run a short checklist. Read the heading and subhead out loud: if you would feel awkward saying them to a real customer, rewrite. Check text contrast on photos. Confirm the WhatsApp number uses a tested wa.me link. Put the primary keyword in the meta title only when the page actually delivers on it. Ask the model to summarize the page in one sentence; if that summary is fuzzy, the layout is still crowded. Save the prompts that worked in their own folder so the next batch—holiday promo, bundle, hiring page—does not start from zero.

An SME landing page in 2026 does not have to be expensive or handed to an agency overnight. What it needs is a sane split of labor: ChatGPT tightens the argument and structure, Gemini handles consistent visual assets, and you protect brand voice and price facts. Start with one offer that is already selling this week, publish, count the chats that come in, then iterate. The tools are already in your pocket. What fewer people do is close the inspiration tabs and hit publish.
