How to Use ChatGPT Sites to Build an Interactive Website without Coding in 2026

ChatGPT Sites is in public beta. Here is how to brief, test, and publish an interactive site without a separate hosting setup.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

For two years many people in Indonesia have been fluent at asking ChatGPT for captions, PDF summaries, or page outlines. The step that still felt distant was making that page actually live—clickable, fillable, and shareable with a client. ChatGPT Sites closes that gap. OpenAI Help, updated in late September 2026, describes Sites as a way to create, preview, publish, and share interactive websites and lightweight apps from Work on the web or from Work and Codex in the desktop app. You do not start in a DNS panel. You start with a sharp brief and the habit of testing the flow before you hit publish.

Read the access rules before you get stuck mid-chat. Sites is in public beta for ChatGPT workspaces plus Plus and Pro accounts. Free and Go are out. In Enterprise workspaces, public publishing is off by default until an admin turns it on. If you cannot see Sites, check three things: you are in the right workspace, the desktop app is not the Classic build, and your admin has not blocked the role. OpenAI suggests starting in Work and writing a request that includes the word “website” or mentions @Sites. Attach content, files, links, and constraints. Without constraints the model tends to build something pretty on a wide screen and awkward on a phone—the device almost every Indonesian prospect uses when they open a WhatsApp link.

Begin with a one-page brief, not a mood board. Write who will use it, the single job the site must finish, the data it may show, and one success action: submit a request, download a schedule, or estimate a package. Briefs that work well include an internal AI request tracker, a new-hire onboarding hub, or a service-package calculator. Paste that brief into a Work chat and ask Sites for a first version with only four blocks: a title and promise, a form or task list, a status or result, and a short privacy note. ChatGPT Learn separates Chat for exploration from Work for finished deliverables. Sites belongs to the second. If you mix a long brainstorm into the same thread, the page usually rambles. Keep research chats and build chats apart.

When the preview appears, do not start polishing color. Test the flow like a rushed user. Submit odd data: an oversized name, a blank number, a broken link. Ask ChatGPT to fix one symptom per message—a dead button, an unclear label, or a result that does not persist. OpenAI Help stresses create, preview, edit, then share. That order is not ceremony. The first version almost always fails on button copy and keyboard focus order. If a team will use the page, share it privately first, ask one colleague to tap every link, and collect numbered notes. Only then consider a public publish. Quotas differ by plan; read the limits shown in your own Sites experience instead of someone else’s social screenshot.

Three traps make a Sites project feel “too AI” and hard for clients to trust. First, a prompt with no real data, so prices and testimonials sound invented. Upload the package list, product photos, or a schedule CSV and tell the model to use those files rather than invent. Second, too many features: chat, gallery, blog, and dashboard in one trial site. OpenAI Academy sessions on building websites with ChatGPT Sites keep returning to a small, focused case. Third, forgetting the interface language. The model defaults to English. State up front that labels, errors, and buttons must be in Indonesian with a shop-floor tone, not American corporate voice. If the visual still feels flat, you can rebuild in a website builder such as Clincoo after you export the text frame—what matters is that the promise and the flow already work.

For freelancers and remote teams, Sites is strongest as a clickable prototype in a meeting, not as the permanent brand home. Keep a folder of briefs that worked: onboarding, calculator, lead form. Each brief names the audience, data limits, and done criteria. Once the client agrees on direction, move final copy, brand color, and the domain onto a stack you control. Microsoft’s 2026 Work Trend Index found that a third of Indonesian workers already count as Frontier Professionals, more than twice the global average. Your audience has seen AI output. They are not impressed by a generator. They are impressed by a page they can finish in three taps.

ChatGPT Sites does not remove information design. It removes the “I have not set up hosting yet” excuse that delays experiments. Use Work, mention @Sites, give a one-job brief, test with ugly data, share privately, then publish if your plan allows it. Save the thread that produced a clean version. Next week, repeat for one other case—do not rebuild an entire site overnight. Interactive websites without coding are already inside ChatGPT in 2026. What is still scarce is someone who closes the inspiration tabs, writes an honest brief, and hits share only after the page can actually be used.
