Practical Flexbox: Arrange Rows and Columns without the Headache

The three Flexbox properties developers use most for headers, cards, and responsive menus.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

Flexbox is the modern way to arrange elements in a row or a column. In CSS, give the parent display: flex. Its children can then be controlled for direction, spacing, and alignment.

The three properties used most often: flex-direction (row or column), justify-content (main axis: space-between, center, etc.), and align-items (cross axis). For a header with logo left and menu right, you usually want display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center.

On small screens, switch direction to column or allow wrapping with flex-wrap: wrap. Test in Clincoo mobile preview. If cards get too narrow, let them drop to the next line.

Do not memorize every value at once. Start with one container, change one property, watch the result. One tidy container is worth more than a layout that almost works everywhere.
