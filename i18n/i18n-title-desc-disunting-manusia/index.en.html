Edit Title and Meta Description per Language; Do Not Rely on Machine Translation

Titles and meta descriptions are not automatic copies. Rewrite them to match language rhythm and SERP length.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

In a Clincoo project that ships both index.html and index.en.html, titles and meta descriptions are often copied then translated word for word. The result feels stiff, too long in one language, and too short in the other. Search engines show those snippets in results, so they need the same care as the page heading.

Start from the page intent, not from the source sentence. If the Indonesian page promises a practical locale tip, the English page should promise the same thing in wording that English readers actually use. Do not force the source clause structure when it sounds odd.

Check length in preview. Comfortable titles usually stay under 60 characters; descriptions under 160. Indonesian often runs longer than English for the same idea, so cut filler. Keep the lang attribute honest, and point canonical at the URL you mean.

After saving, open both files side by side in the Clincoo editor. Read the titles out loud. If they sound machine-translated, rewrite them. One human pass beats a cheap impression in the browser tab and on share cards.

Spend two minutes per bilingual page. That is cheaper than fixing rankings after an awkward snippet is already indexed.
