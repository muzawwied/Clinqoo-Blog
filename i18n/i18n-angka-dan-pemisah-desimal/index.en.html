Format Numbers and Decimal Separators to Match the Page Locale

Dots and commas swap meaning across languages. Use Intl.NumberFormat instead of hardcoded separators.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

Indonesian readers treat 1.250 as one thousand two hundred fifty; English readers treat it as a fraction. Sharing raw digits across ID and EN pages invites price and stats mistakes.

Use Intl.NumberFormat with the page locale: id-ID for comma decimals and dot thousands, en-US for the inverse. Do not hardcode separators in a Clincoo template.

Keep the formatter locale aligned with the html lang attribute. index.html uses id-ID; index.en.html uses en-US. Do not paste one call into both files.

Test three cases: a large integer, a two-digit fraction, and zero. Preview both languages before deploy so price cards stay honest.

Clincoo serves static HTML. Format numbers in the copy source or with a small script that reads document lang, not by editor guesswork.
