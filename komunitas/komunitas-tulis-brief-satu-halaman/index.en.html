Write a One-Page Brief before Inviting Contributors to a Clincoo Project

An invite without a brief lets people touch color, copy, and structure at once. One page of intent saves review time.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

A Clincoo community moves faster when people know what they may change. A one-page brief — site goal, audience, CTA, locked colors, and which pages are in play — stops contributors from quietly rewriting the brand.

Write the brief in plain sentences, not a long spec. Example: The home page should explain the service in two sentences and point to the form. Do not change the palette. About is still a draft; proposals are welcome.

Attach the preview URL and one screenshot of the current state. Without that, people review their memory instead of the live Clincoo subdomain.

Also write what you will not do this week. That boundary matters as much as the task list. A finished logo debate should not reopen just because someone new joined the chat.

Keep the brief in the repo or the same project note as the changelog. When a decision changes, edit the brief instead of relying on a buried thread.

When you invite, send the brief first, then ask for a pull request or an edit. Three minutes of reading saves a night of back-and-forth comments.
