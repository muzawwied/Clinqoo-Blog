Use Easing, Not Linear, for Clincoo UI Motion

Linear transitions make menus and buttons feel mechanical. Prefer ease-out for enter motion.

This article is available in: Indonesian · English

A linear animation moves values at a constant speed. In a UI that makes dropdowns and drawers feel like machines, not objects that come to rest.

For elements entering the screen, use ease-out: fast at the start, slow at the end. For elements leaving, ease-in often feels more natural.

Avoid extreme cubic-bezier bounce on primary buttons. Bounce fits a small success cue, not navigation.

Keep duration and easing consistent across the same component family — navbar, modal, tooltip — so the site does not feel like a pile of demos.

Honor prefers-reduced-motion: if the user asks for less motion, cut transitions to zero or very short, even the ones with good easing.

Test in Clincoo preview on a phone. Easing that looks smooth on desktop can feel sluggish above 300ms.
