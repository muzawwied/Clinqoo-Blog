Using Your Own Domain: DNS Basics for Site Owners

A custom domain makes your site sound serious. Here is what to know before using one.

This article is available in: Indonesia · English

A freshly deployed site gets a default address — handy for sharing with friends. But once your site gets serious, your own domain like yourname.com adds a professional, memorable touch.

It works simply: you buy a domain from a domain registrar, then connect it to your hosting through DNS settings — the internet’s phone book that tells every visitor where to be sent. DNS changes usually take effect within minutes to a few hours.

What beginners must understand: a domain and hosting are two separate things. The domain is the address, hosting is the house. You can replace either one without touching the other — your site does not disappear.

If you feel unsure, use the default address first and move to a custom domain once your site gains regular visitors. No rule says rush — what matters is that your site is live and keeps growing.
